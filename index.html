The Platform

Four Concrete Commitments.

Non-negotiable bylaws designed to dismantle coercion, protect whistleblowers, and bring total transparency to student life at Ashoka.

01
The Confidential Whistleblower Line

Fear of social retaliation keeps students silent. We establish an ironclad, untraceable reporting channel directly to the student representative.

Complainants receive private cryptographic tracking tokens with zero university ERP or Ashoka email logging. Any attempt by seniors or accused parties to confront or ostracize a complainant carries immediate residential campus suspension.

02
24-Hour Mandatory Hearing Clause

Justice delayed is intimidation multiplied. When an incident occurs in residence corridors (RH1–RH5) or off-campus shuttles, action cannot wait weeks.

Preliminary committee proceedings must formally open within 24 hours of submission. Includes immediate, discreet alternative room relocation within 3 hours if a student feels unsafe in their hall.

03
Clean Inductions & O-Week Reform

Hazing too often hides behind the mask of "society traditions" or "sports team bonding." We draw a non-negotiable boundary.

All club tryouts, dance inductions, and athletic rosters must publish Dean-approved charters. Closed-door humiliation rituals result in immediate society de-recognition. Anti-ragging bylaws explicitly extend to dhaba visits and off-campus gatherings.

04
The Open Record: Public Accountability

The Anti-Ragging Committee cannot remain a black box. The student body deserves full visibility on grievance resolutions.

Publishing bi-monthly anonymized resolution metrics—tracking report counts, turnaround times, and corrective actions taken so students know their voices bring real outcomes.

Candidate Dispatch
"Respect is never coerced. A university must be a space of fearless curiosity—never one where students endure intimidation disguised as tradition."

For two years, I have watched peers hesitate to report coercive behavior because they feared social fallout, whisper networks, or administrative apathy. The Anti-Ragging Committee cannot just be a passive email address buried in a handbook. It must be an active, uncompromising student shield.

Whether it's subtle pressure during club tryouts, corridor bullying in residence halls, or off-campus hazing, no student should ever have to normalize harassment to belong. I am standing to ensure that every Ashokan is protected, heard, and backed by swift, decisive action.
